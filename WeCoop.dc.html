Pricing and Proposal Creation System for Designers

Context

WeCoop is a B2B platform where companies work with designers: clients post projects, receive proposals, select a designer, and negotiate project terms within the platform.

For the MVP, we needed to create a pricing system that could serve as the foundation for estimating project costs.

Challenge

Design a pricing system from scratch that clearly explains how the final project cost is calculated and generates a structured cost breakdown for accounting purposes.

This is especially important for tenders, where a designer’s proposal needs to provide a transparent breakdown by type and volume of work.

Constraints

WeCoop minimizes direct communication between clients and designers, so key agreements need to happen through structured interactions within the product.

The price list also needs to be reusable when creating proposals. At the same time, the MVP has to stay relatively simple while collecting enough data to inform future product decisions.

As a result, the first version includes steps whose immediate value may not always be obvious to designers—for example, estimating different types of work by complexity and time. This data will help us understand which parameters actually affect pricing.

My Role

I analyzed business requirements, designed user flows and product logic, presented and defended design decisions, supported implementation, and conducted design reviews.

I worked in a cross-functional team with an Art Director, Stakeholder, Product Manager, Frontend and Backend Engineers, and QA.

Price Adjustment

User interviews showed that the cost of a design project cannot always be calculated simply by adding up fixed service prices. An unclear brief, complex approval processes, and other risks can significantly affect the final estimate.

To account for this, I introduced an uncertainty coefficient: the system calculates a base price from the designer’s price list, while the designer can adjust it to reflect the context of a specific project.

Deadline Negotiation

Discussing deadlines in a chat would go against WeCoop’s approach to minimizing direct communication, so I designed a structured negotiation flow using a calendar.

The client proposes a deadline, the designer can suggest a different date, and both sides continue adjusting it until they reach an agreement.

This replaces an open-ended conversation with a focused interaction built around a single parameter—the project deadline.

What’s Next

The price list has been launched as part of the MVP, so the next iterations will be driven by real usage data.

I’ll compare prices saved in designers’ price lists with prices in actual proposals and track how often designers adjust the price calculated by the system. This will help determine how closely the current structure reflects real-world pricing models and which parameters actually influence estimates.

Based on these findings, I’ll be able to remove redundant parameters, simplify the flow, and improve pricing accuracy while keeping the cost breakdown transparent for clients.
